# Module 1


## Module Overview
### Key objectives
By the end of this module, you will be able to do the following:

Compare and contrast Western and Indigenous worldviews and their impacts on business and society
Describe the idea of two-eyed seeing as perspective that decision-makers can embrace
Explain how business and society has come to approach the notion of value and why it poses a challenge
Define business sustainability and distinguish between it and other related terms (corporate social responsibility, triple bottom line, and shared value)

If you pause to think critically about how the world is operated, you'll notice that the
economic systems that have dominated society have operated in a way that have
created value and created value added, by one group in society going out into the
world, finding and discovering resources, or more aptly, exploiting resources such
as cheap labor or natural resources, most often from other communities, and
bringing them back to their own communities to create value added for their own,
rather than contributing to the communities that they're extracting resources from.

Much of the so-called “value creation” that we hear about extracts value from one group or place for the benefit of others.

As Marianna Mazzucato discusses, there has been very little emphasis on re-investment in the community.

And so she states—and, remember, this is one of the leading economists in the world emphasizing this—“Let’s make sure that we’re not also confusing value extraction with value creation.”

Sustainability

We know of the state of the world. Just take a look outside. And in the context of it,
we often hear this term business sustainability. But what does this actually mean?
We already established that the purpose of a business is to create value, at least in
today's world. What about sustainability?
00:25 - 01:12
Most people in the world draw on the UN Brundtland Commission, and tell us that
sustainability is really about meeting the needs of the present without
compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. So, taken
together, as Tima Bansal, a pioneer in this space states “Business sustainability
means that businesses should create wealth (which is the aggregate of value) and
make people's lives better. But, they need to do so without compromising the health
and the wealth of future generations… The long term goal is thriving societies and
ecosystems.
” So that’s business sustainability.

What Mariana Mazzucato has brilliantly argued and enlightened the world with is

01:59 - 02:42
The overall consensus amongst experts in the area of business sustainability and
responsible business is that how we're approaching things just isn't working.
Changes have been incremental at best. The social and ecological issues that we
see in society, they're persisting and in many cases they're getting worse because
we're failing to fundamentally change things. A major shift is needed in how we think
and how we do things. What's good, though, is that we're seeing hints of businesses
starting to change. What's also fantastic is that many of the ideas that we need to
be engaging with already exist.

How we have been approaching businesses and the notion of value creation is not working.

Business sustainability means:

 
“...businesses should create wealth and make people’s lives better. But, they need to do so without compromising the health and wealth of future generations … The long-term goal is thriving societies and ecosystems.”
— Bansal, 2022Links to an external site.
Over the past twenty years or so, “business sustainability” has often been used ambiguously with and confused with other ideas that are related to sustainability but remain distinct from it. These include:

Corporate social responsibility (CSR): Behave “responsibly”
Triple bottom line: Consider social, environmental, and financial bottom lines
Shared value: Simultaneously create value for businesses and society
Our approach to pursuing business sustainability also hasn’t been working. Changes have been incremental and/or refashioned back into the existing way of doing business (e.g., “business as usual”) that has created today’s challenges. As Bansal further notes:

 
“No single business can be sustainable – only groups of organizations.”
— Bansal, 2022Links to an external site.
A major shift in thinking and ways of being is needed.
