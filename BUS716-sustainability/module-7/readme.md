The idea of innovation is inescapable in business and society. Innovation is an exciting and powerful force shaping modern business and society, yet its impacts can vary dramatically depending on how it is conceived and implemented.

Two contrasting narratives emerge when examining the innovation landscape: the promise of innovation that benefits both businesses and society, and the reality that innovations can lead to exploitation and disruption, both social and environmental.

### What is sustainable innovation?
There are many different definitions of innovation, but the essence of it centers around the introduction of something that is relatively new or novel. Let’s stress “relatively” here since it is difficult to judge whether something is actually new. If you take a look at many of the innovations gaining lots of attention, you will notice that they are often actually ideas that already existed elsewhere and were imported into a different context.

In this sense, many experts view one important form of innovation as the travel and translation of ideas into new contexts. The slides below give some examples of this movement.

Sustainable innovation involves making intentional changes to a company’s products, services, or processes to generate long-term social and environmental benefits while creating economic profits for the firm.
— Lee, 2021Links to an external site.
There are many terms out there, such as “social innovation” and “eco innovation.” While there are subtle differences between these concepts, they all deal with change and the introduction of new ideas with positive social or environmental benefits. “Sustainable innovation” captures the essence of such terms.

What’s critical to recognize is that the idea of sustainable innovation can be applied in a range of organizational contexts as explained in the table below, adapted from the typology of social innovation developed by Tracey and Stott (2017)Links to an external site..

| | Sustainable Entrepreneurship | Sustainable Intrapreneurship | Sustainable Extraprenseurship |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Definition | The process of creating and growing a venture, either for-profit or non-profit, where the motivation of the entrepreneur is to address social challenges | The process of addressing social challenges from inside established organizations | The process of interorganizational action that facilitates alternative combinations of ideas, people, places and resources to address social challenges | 
| Approach to sustainability | Creates change through the founding of new organizations | Creates change by leveraging the resources and capabilities of established organizations | Creates change through platforms that support collective effort within and between new and established organizations |

## The Need for Sustainable Innovation

The need for sustainable innovation is very apparent if we just pause and think

the world. If we want to create a socially and environmentally sustainable society,

At the end of the day, there are many different reasons on why we need sustainable
innovation and why it can benefit organizations and society. Two key ones, however,
are, first: new ideas really can enhance our ability to address sustainability issues;
and second: many existing innovations and past innovations have actually created
or exacerbated sustainability issues, making conditions worse for one community,

If the goal is to contribute towards environmental and social sustainability, it is important to think critically about how and which new ideas are implemented as innovation in itself will not address today’s pressing problems.

At the heart of sustainable innovation is the explicit consideration of the externalities of an idea.

Ultimately, recognizing that businesses affect and are affected by society and the environment, sustainable innovation is needed for two important reasons: (1) new ideas can enhance our ability to address sustainability issues; and (2) many innovations today create and/or exacerbate sustainability issues.

Let’s look at each of these two drivers in more detail.

### Addressing sustainability issues through innovation
Our ability to address today’s pressing issues (e.g., climate change, social inequality) can be enhanced through the introduction and scaling of new ideas that directly tackle them. This is true for both technological innovations as well as social and cultural innovations, particularly since tackling many issues hinges on changing behaviours.

### Addressing traditional innovation and sustainability issues
Innovations often create or exacerbate social and/or environmental issues in the pursuit of profit. While they may create value for one community, it is often done so at the cost of another, illustrating the idea of value extraction or destruction as economist Mariana Mazzucato has argued.

 
Let’s make sure that we’re not also confusing value extraction with value creation.
— Mariana Mazzucato (TED, 2020Links to an external site.)
Developing innovations that serve key populations in need is an important part of the intersection of sustainability and innovation. For example, organizations working to develop and scale various access to energy solutions in rural and underserved areas are addressing an important issue in the pursuit for a just and equity society where everyone’s basic needs are met.

However, an unfortunate reality is that in today's business environment, innovation often seems to be pursued and praised for the sake of innovation to the point where it seems as though we’ve lost sight of goals such as creating new ideas that benefit society. Researchers Lam and Seidel address this in their discussion of hypergrowth.

 
Young potential entrepreneurs talk about exit strategies before even creating an innovation or starting a business. Our innovation ecosystems push them to do so in many ways. Seemingly straightforward questions to budding entrepreneurs such as “What is your exit strategy?” drastically shift focus and outcomes away from creating long–term societally beneficial innovations. … this hypergrowth exit mindset is destroying societal wellbeing due to its laser focus on increasing socially constructed exit value above all else.
— Lam & Seidel, 2020Links to an external site.
Whether or not it is intentional, the negative externalities of innovations are often justified or overlooked. Indeed, many of the examples that we’ve already explored so far actually exemplify how negative externalities and exploitative nature of innovation or what some people call the dark side of innovation.

## Closing Thoughts

Innovation is really exciting. I've spent the bulk of my career, in academia and in
industry, working at this intersection of social change and innovation. And if I've
learned anything, it's really that we need to recognize that innovation in itself is not
going to save the world. We need to be intentional and explicit about considering
the social and environmental impacts of innovation, whether or not we're directly
targeting a sustainability related issue.
00:34 - 01:13
When thinking about an innovation, take a step back and try to look at the whole
impact, the bigger picture; direct and indirect impacts, intentional and
unintentional throughout the entire lifecycle of it. We could spend days and months
talking about the innovation process and how to create innovative ideas. But at the
end of the day, with sustainable innovation, one of the simplest and most effective
things that you can do is just consider the positive and negative externalities of any
idea that you're looking at. This small step can really go a long way.

Sustainable innovation is really about approaching innovation in a way that not only creates positive social and environmental externalities but also minimizes, or ideally, eliminates the negative externalities associated with it. It requires you to consider both the social and environmental impacts of an idea while embracing systems thinking to recognize the direct and indirect—as well as the intended and unintended—consequences of an idea over time.

While there are many different ways that sustainable innovation can be pursued, a simple but effective way is to intentionally and explicitly consider the positive and negative externalities of any given innovation.

For example, the Business Model Canvas is one of the most widely used frameworks for entrepreneurs and innovators when they are thinking about creating a new venture. However, what is distinctively missing from it is a place for you to explore externalities. We can see with tools such as the Sustainable Business Model CanvasLinks to an external site. that by simply integrating consideration of externalities into the innovation process, we can set ourselves up to better innovate sustainably.
