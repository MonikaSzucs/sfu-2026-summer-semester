# Quiz 1

1. A furniture company designs its own sofas, manufactures them in its own factories, and sells and delivers them only through its own website and warehouses, with no outside producers involved. This business is best characterized as:
- A pipeline

2.  A niche online marketplace for handmade jewelry has plenty of buyers and sellers registering, but very few transactions close, because buyers can't tell which sellers are trustworthy and search results are mostly irrelevant to what they typed. This is best described as:
- An interaction failure

3. A grocery chain lets independent local farmers list and sell produce directly to shoppers through the chain's app, with the chain earning a commission and providing delivery logistics. This move shifts the chain toward platform thinking primarily because it:
- Repositions the firm as infrastructure that enables outside producers to reach consumers directly

4. On a ride-hailing app, more drivers joining shortens wait times for riders, and more riders joining makes the app more worthwhile for drivers to keep open. This is an example of:
- A cross-side network effect

5. A software platform becomes more valuable to users as more third-party developers build apps for it — and a larger user base, in turn, attracts more developers. Class materials describe this "bigger catalogue pulls in the other side" dynamic as:
- An indirect network effect

6. A messaging app pays $5 per successful referral and sees a huge signup spike, but usage doesn't compound in value as more people join, and churn stays high. The most accurate read is:
- This is viral growth — it can drive fast acquisition but isn't the same as a defensible network effect

7. A new two-sided freelance marketplace launches with zero freelancers and zero clients. Before opening client signups, the founders manually source and post 200 freelancer profiles themselves. This is a strategy to:
- Solve the chicken-and-egg problem by seeding one side of the market first

8. The Van Alstyne / Choudary framework describes a platform's "core interaction" using three building blocks. Which set is correct?
- Participants, value unit, and filter

9. On a recipe app, user-submitted recipes and photos attract new visitors through search and social sharing, and those new visitors go on to submit their own recipes, growing the catalogue further. This loop is best described as:
- A UGC flywheel that compounds catalogue growth and discovery

10. A small edtech startup built its entire product on top of a large platform's API and distribution channel. The large platform later changes its algorithm and API terms overnight, cutting the startup's traffic in half. This illustrates:
- Ecosystem dependency risk
