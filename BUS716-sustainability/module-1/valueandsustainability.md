## Knowledge and Knowledge Systems
With that said, how do we develop our knowledge and understanding of the world? Each of us has a slightly different worldview and comes at our knowledge and understanding of the world differently.

Our knowledge accumulates over time and structures how we think and interact with society and the planet.

It’s important to recognize that different knowledge systems or forms of knowledge exist, including these two types:

1. Indigenous/traditional
2. Western

In Western societies, our knowledge systems and knowledge of the world (e.g., what we learn in school, and how we are taught to think) are very much anchored in what we call “science” and “rationality.” The ideas we engage with are often portrayed and learned in abstract and decontextualized manners. In itself, this is neither good nor bad.

But what’s important to recognize is that this knowledge system has come to be part of our lives only because of centuries of colonization where Western (European) worldviews and knowledge were forced upon people by power and violence in an attempt to dominate, take resources, and control Indigenous Peoples that have been taking care of the Land for millennia.

Beyond the Western knowledge system that we all know are Indigenous knowledge systems (also called traditional knowledge systems) located all around the world. While they differ from place to place and are highly contextualized and specific to the locality, they tend to embrace a holistic and relational grounding between people, the land, and all objects, which is missing from Western knowledge systems.

Indigenous knowledge has been created and maintained through generations of observations, lived experiences, oral history, and community-centred approaches to serve local communities.

“There are many cases where science and history are catching up with what Indigenous peoples have long known.”
— Nicholas, 2018Links to an external site.

Example: The Potlatch (Means “To Give”)

### The Roots of Today’s Challenges
Many of the core ideas that we embrace in the economy and society are based on Western knowledge and worldviews that are at the roots of pressing challenges today.
