## Unit Grouping
- Unit Grouping is a central aspect of how organizations are designed
- Once an organization is too large to manage as a single group, people need to be grouped into units for administrative purposes.
- This designates formal reporting relationships, increases specialization and clarifies accountabilities.
- The basic rule of thumb is that communication and coordination occurs better within group boundaries than across boundaries.
-  Groups have a natural tendency to identify themselves by differentiating from other groups, creating we-they dynamics and competition
- Grouping encourages coordination within the group at the expense of possible lack of coordination between groups
- Logic dictates that you group people who must manage critical interdependencies first
- Much of the action in organization restructuring is changing unit grouping

## Functional Unit Grouping grouping people by the function they perform

![](/BUS0719-managing-people-and-organizations/module-3/img/functional_unit_grouping.png)

- what are the strengths
- what are the drawbacks?

Strengths
- economies of scale
- in-depth skill development
- encourages professional orientation
- faster diffusion of technical innovations

Weaknesses
- Fragmentation, conflict and poor integration
- Lose sight of overall goals
- Slow response to change
- Requires Tops to manage lower level conflicts

Uses
- When human resources are scarce
- When efficiency is the key strategic issue
- When the need for adaptability is low
- When the need for continuous development of specialized expertise is high
- When the need for coordination between functions is low

## Product Unit Grouping grouping people by the product or service they deliver
- what are the strengths?
- what are the drawbacks?

Strengths
- Greater focus on product and customer
- Good coordination across different specialization's
- Faster product innovation
- Faster response to change

Weaknesses
- Lose economies of scale in functions
- Reduces in-depth competence of specialists and can reduce their morale
- Slower to diffuse functional innovations
- Makes standardization across product lines difficult

Uses
- When product innovation is the key strategic issue
- When the level of environmental change is medium
- When human resources are not scarce
- When products are relatively independent

## Market Grouping grouping people by the market they serve

![](/BUS0719-managing-people-and-organizations/module-3/img/market_grouping.png)

- what are the advantages
- what are the drawbacks?

Strengths
- Focuses on customers
- Allows for integration across product lines
- Contains interdependencies within relatively independent units
- Faster at strategic change

Weaknesses
- Larger size makes it difficult to adapt to local conditions
- Less innovation in specific products
- moderate reduction in technical specialization
- low diffusion of innovations across units

Uses
- When groups of products are intertwined
- When the level of environmental change is medium to high
- When customer needs are the strategic issue
- When integration of products or technologies is needed to meet customer needs

## Admin Matrix
- is an attempt to put both a functional and product or market or regional press on managers based on the theory that the better matched the design is to its environment, the more effective the organization. Design the competing values/polarities/strategic priorities into the organization’s structure.
- Matrixed managers must be good at claiming, negotiating, giving and receiving authority
- both types of grouping must be similarly influential, authoritative, powerful
- has had poor success in America, but is a common form of organization at senior levels of large European companies

## Project Matrix
| | Chief Engineer Civil | Chief Engineer Mechanical | Chief Engineer Hydrolic |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| PM project 1 | 5 | 4.5 | .5 |
| PM project 2 | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| PM project 3 | 3.5 | 3 | 1 |
| Employee Pools | 15 | 12 | 4 |

- advantage?
- drawbacks?

- Very common in engineering firms
- Pools of functional expertise combine and disband around specific projects
- Usually project management is itself a function with a boss
- Specialists report to both a functional manager and a project manager
- Individuals may be on more than one project at a time

Strengths
- Can achieve a balance of functional and product emphases
- Can balance specialization and product knowledge
- Balances HR efficiencies and innovation
- Flexible sharing of human resources
- Able to quickly adapt to change
- Functional innovations diffuse quickly

Weaknesses
- Requires balanced power of functional and project leaders
- requires a great deal of coordination time
- requires higher level interpersonal and team skills
- can create peaks and valleys of productivity as teams form and disband
- dual authority can be stressful and frustrating
- project teams can lose sight of financial control
- same high performers wanted on all projects

Uses
- When flexible provision of technical expertise is the key strategic issue
- When products/services are changing rapidly
- When there is a scarcity of technical expertise

## Networked, Empowered, Cluster fluid grouping
- One large self-contained group with all the human, capital and material resources to run the own business. Teams form and disband in response to customer needs, organizational needs or new opportunities.
- Sometimes shared services like HR and Digital Media are cost centers, sometimes they have to sell their services to internal customers or are outsourced.
- Common in start-ups. Used in large organizations to introduce or maintain the entrepreneurial zeal of startups.
- Internal leveraged buy-outs are sometimes used to set this up. “Sell” the business to employees for current net worth. Instead of budgets, the unit has a monthly loan payment to make.

## grouping by clusters
Strengths
- encourages efficiency and innovation
- highly adaptable and flexible
- very innovative at developing new business opportunities
- suited to many stand-alone businesses
- When there is high equity position of employees, little control or other incentives needed

Weaknesses
- Does not seem to work with organizations much over 150 employees (Dunbar’s number)
- confusing for people used to working in more structured organizations
- little diffusion of innovations across clusters
- can lead to different clusters competing for the same clients

Uses
- When motivation and entrepreneurism of employees is the key strategic issue
- when it is possible to maintain small independent business units
- when there is a high need for flexibility and continuous adaptation

## Contingencies Influencing Org Design
In understanding the impact of the environment on organization design
there are two controversies

1. Are organizations driven by environmental forces or by managerial
choices?

2. Do organizational forms depend primarily on resource constraints or
information uncertainty?

## Integrating These Perspectives
Lawrence & Dyer
- The environment contains two types of uncertainty:

1. Information Complexity
- The rate of change in the environment (technical, political, competitive, customer preferences, social)
- Can we predict what will be happening in the relevant future?
- Do we know what to do?

2. Resource Scarcity
- availability or substitutability of resources (human, financial, material) necessary for the organization’s functioning.
- Can we get what we need?
