### Tempered radicals and strategies for social change
Organizational change, especially when it relates to organizational culture, is notoriously difficult. Yet, the path towards a sustainable society, including sustainable businesses runs through culture change. While science tells us what needs to happen from a technical standpoint, culture determines whether it actually will happen.

Research on organizational change and change agents specifically in the context of sustainability overwhelmingly stresses how successful change requires patience, strategic thinking, and the ability to work within existing structures rather than against them.

Rather than seeking blatant cultural change, consider how you can leverage existing culture to pursue social change efforts. If you are motivated to pursue a sustainability issue and want to see sustainability further embedded within your organization, consider how you can be a “Tempered Radical.”

[Pitching for Social Change](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cf225i2oDbM)

While there are an array of strategies out there, many of them ultimately stress the following approaches. These approaches demonstrate that sustainable social change emerges through strategic persistence, relationship-building, and the ability to frame progressive values in terms that resonate with existing organizational priorities and business imperatives.

Select the tabs below to read more about each approach to change agency.

> Know your environment
Effective change agents must thoroughly understand their business context, connecting sustainability ideas to existing business strategy rather than proposing entirely new directions. This credibility is essential for gaining trust and influence.

> Start small, build momentum
Tempered radicals leverage "small wins": incremental changes that create positive momentum without triggering organizational resistance. These small actions can ripple into significant transformations through what researchers call "deviation amplification."

> Strategic timing and patience
Successful change agents know when to bring ideas forward and when to wait, often preparing for crisis moments when organizations become more receptive to change. Political opportunities or threats often create windows for collective action.

> Build collective power
Individual actions have limitations. Collective action provides greater legitimacy, power, and resources than individual efforts, transforming isolated "tokens" into influential minorities. Change agents must demonstrate consistent commitment to the business while keeping their initiatives from being perceived as personal pet projects.

> Balance passion with pragmatism
Effective change agents harness their passion while keeping emotions in check, presenting logical business cases rather than emotional appeals. They practice "disruptive self-expression"—subtle actions that challenge assumptions without directly threatening the status quo.

### Internal organizational action
Within organizations, storytelling builds crucial buy-in by humanizing abstract sustainability goals. When leaders share stories about how environmental initiatives affect real employees, communities, or customers, they transform sustainability from a compliance checkbox into a meaningful mission. Stories help employees understand their role in larger change efforts, creating shared purpose and collective identity around sustainability values.


Effective internal storytelling also overcomes resistance by connecting sustainability to existing business priorities. Rather than presenting environmental initiatives as additional burdens, compelling narratives demonstrate how sustainability efforts align with profitability, innovation, and employee satisfaction.

As managers and members of organizations, consider the following best practices when engaging in storytelling from within. Select the tabs below to read more.

> Authenticity first
Share genuine stories from real experiences rather than manufactured marketing messages. Authentic narratives build credibility and trust.

> Focus on people
Center stories around human impact—employees, customers, or community members—rather than abstract metrics or policies.

> Create concrete visuals
Use specific details and imagery that help audiences visualize outcomes and consequences.

> Connect to values
Link sustainability stories to existing organizational or personal values that resonate with your audience.

> Call to action
Every story should include clear, achievable next steps that enable audiences to participate in the change effort.

### External stakeholder engagement
Externally, storytelling helps organizations communicate their sustainability commitments authentically to consumers, partners, and the public. Stories about sustainable practices, community impact, or environmental stewardship create brand differentiation and build trust with increasingly conscious consumers. Partners and investors respond to narratives that demonstrate long-term thinking and stakeholder value creation.


Stories often succeed because they engage emotions alongside logic. Framing sustainability challenges through personal narratives, community impacts, or future scenarios helps audiences viscerally understand consequences and opportunities. This emotional engagement catalyzes action by making abstract issues personally relevant and urgent.

Visuals have been known to be a particularly powerful tool. Organizations using visual storytelling should recognize that visuals alone are not always effective.

The following practices summarize recommendations based on the latest research on using visuals to stimulate social change. Select each tab to read more.

> Transform negative emotions into action
Shocking images alone can overwhelm audiences and cause disengagement. Effective change agents convert initial guilt, blame, and moral shock into positive emotional energy (e.g., hope, empowerment) that drives action.

> Create three key connections
Use powerful symbols to connect viewers with 1 the cause itself; 2 a collective identity and community; and 3 the organization leading the change. This builds trust, belonging, and agency.

> Build community
Transform individual guilt into collective purpose by helping people feel part of a movement working together to solve the problem. Position supporters as part of a vanguard creating positive change.

> Establish organizational credibility
Organizations must be seen as trustworthy and effective leaders to successfully channel emotional energy into sustained behavioural change.
